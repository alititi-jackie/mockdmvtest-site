The two parts of the driver's license test

In the U.S., the driver's license test has two parts. First the written (knowledge) test for a learner's permit, then the road test for the full license. You must pass the written part before you can schedule the road part.

Part 1: the written test

A multiple-choice exam on traffic laws, road signs, and safe driving — 20–50 questions depending on the state, about 80% correct to pass. Prepare with DMV written test questions and answers by topic, then take timed practice tests until you score 90%+ twice in a row.

Part 2: the road test

A 15–20 minute drive with an examiner scoring your observation habits, speed control, intersection handling, and parking — including parallel parking in most states. The most common failures aren't technical: skipping blind-spot checks, rolling through stop signs, and speeding even slightly.

  1. Book ahead. Popular DMV locations can be weeks out. Morning weekday slots have lighter traffic.
  2. Bring a roadworthy car. The examiner checks lights, horn, brakes, and signals first — a fault can cancel the test.
  3. Exaggerate observation. Turn your head for blind spots, check mirrors constantly. Make it visible.

Typical timeline

Study 2–4 weeks for the written test, practice driving 1–3 months on your permit (many states require logged supervised hours), then take the road test. Rushing either stage is the top reason people retake.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between the written test and the road test?

The written test is a multiple-choice knowledge exam for a learner's permit; the road test is an in-car driving exam for the full license. You take the written test first.

How long does it take to get a driver's license?

Typically 2–6 months: a few weeks of study, then 1–3 months of supervised practice on a learner's permit before the road test.

Can I take the driver's license test in Chinese?

The written test is offered in Chinese in many states (NY, CA, NJ, PA, MA, WA, TX, FL). The road test is conducted in English with simple instructions.

What should I bring to the road test?

Your learner's permit, proof of identity, vehicle registration and insurance, and a car in good working order.

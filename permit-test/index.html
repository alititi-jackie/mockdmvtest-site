What is a permit test?

A permit test is the knowledge exam you take to earn a learner's permit — the provisional license that lets you practice driving with a licensed adult. It is the same exam most people call the DMV written test or knowledge test.

Passing the permit test doesn't give you a full license. It gives you supervised driving privileges while you prepare for the road test.

Steps to get your learner's permit

  1. Study your state's handbook. Every question on the permit test comes from the official driver handbook. Read it once, cover to cover.
  2. Take permit practice tests. Work by topic first (signs, right-of-way, speed), then take full timed simulations. Target 90%+ twice in a row.
  3. Gather documents. Proof of identity, residency, and legal presence. Requirements vary by state — check the DMV checklist before you go.
  4. Visit the DMV. Take the test, pass a vision screening, pay the fee, and get your permit the same day in most states.

Tips to pass on your first try

  1. Learn the numbers. Following distances, BAC limits, parking distances, and speed limits in school zones are the most-missed questions.
  2. Read every option. Permit tests love "all of the above" and near-identical distractors. Slow down on the last five questions.
  3. Don't overthink. Your first instinct on road-sign questions is usually right. Flag hard ones and return if time allows.

Frequently asked questions

How old do you have to be to take the permit test?

Most states allow it at 15–16, with some variation. A few states have graduated licensing with extra restrictions for drivers under 18.

Is the permit test the same as the written test?

Yes — "permit test," "written test," and "knowledge test" all refer to the same multiple-choice exam for a learner's permit.

How long is a learner's permit valid?

Typically 1–2 years depending on the state, with required supervised driving hours before you can take the road test.

Can I drive alone with a learner's permit?

No. You must drive with a licensed adult (usually 21+) in the front seat, and some states add nighttime and passenger restrictions.
